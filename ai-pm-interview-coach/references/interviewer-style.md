# Interviewer-style inference

Use this reference alongside [real-interview review](interview-review.md). Infer only observable interviewing preferences; do not diagnose personality, invent motives, or treat one early signal as fixed style.

## Evidence levels and confidence

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Observed | Directly present in the transcript or successfully analyzed audio. |
| Inferred | Plausible interpretation grounded in observed evidence. |
| Unknown | The modality or evidence is insufficient. |

Every style conclusion must contain these labeled fields: **Evidence level: Observed | Inferred | Unknown**; **Evidence:** timestamps or turn references; **Competing interpretation:**; **Confidence:** high, medium, or low; **Candidate adjustment:**; and **Anti-overfit warning:**. Mark interviewer tone, vocal affect, and other vocal conclusions **Unknown** when only text is available.

## Observable signals

Inspect, where visible:

- question breadth versus depth;
- interruptions, follow-up sequence, and challenge pattern;
- repeated demands for numbers, ownership, process, trade-offs, technical detail, or strategic framing;
- acceptance of vague answers versus requests for clarification or proof;
- ordering of resume, project, scenario, and motivation questions;
- candidate-question time and the interviewer’s response behavior.

Only after successfully analyzed audio may the analysis add speaking rate, audible pauses, overlap, intonation, or vocal affect. Name the audio operation that produced the evidence. A transcript annotation can support an observed textual pause or interruption, but not an audible-pause, tone, or speaking-rate finding.

## Provisional live adaptation card

After the first three to five substantive questions—or the earliest point that evidence allows—produce a provisional card:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Likely evaluation preference | Evidence-grounded hypothesis, or Unknown. |
| Answer length | Short, medium, or detailed, with observed basis. |
| Level of detail | Which evidence types to lead with. |
| Opening structure | The recommended first sentence and answer order. |
| What to test next | A reversible adjustment and the next interviewer reaction that would confirm or weaken it. |

Low evidence requires a low-confidence card and continued observation. Do not force a conclusion merely because three to five questions have elapsed. Later conflicting behavior must revise the card rather than being ignored.

## Interaction timeline and adaptation review

Organize the interaction chronologically around actual phases, such as opening calibration, resume verification, core-project depth, capability challenge, role fit, and candidate questions. Mark evidence of trust increasing, risk emerging, unresolved doubts, and the earliest observable style signal.

For a completed interview, state when the candidate could reasonably have formed an initial hypothesis, whether they adjusted answer length, order, or evidence type, whether that adjustment worked, and what should change next time. Keep the conclusion conditional: a preference for concise answers may also reflect time pressure; deeper follow-ups may reflect a single project’s ambiguity. Preserve these competing explanations in the report.
