# Longitudinal analysis and later-round preparation

Use this reference only for a longitudinal review of explicitly selected historical evidence or for a later-round preparation request that explicitly asks to use selected earlier real-interview evidence. Read the [input contract](input-contract.md) first; use [real-interview review](interview-review.md) and [interviewer-style inference](interviewer-style.md) for interpretation of earlier real interviews, and [preparation and simulation](preparation-and-simulation.md) for current-round question design.

## Scope, evidence, and unknowns

State the supplied scope before analysis. Analyze only the exact files, attachments, or folder scope the user explicitly selected. A folder mention never authorizes reading an adjacent or parent directory; do not search for presumed earlier rounds. If an explicitly selected folder is the scope, enumerate the included artifacts before analysis and do not extend outside it. Do not infer interview order from filename order, directory order, or file timestamps.

Start with an evidence register that lists every included artifact and its: exact designation; interview identity; company; role; stage; date; modality; real/simulation status; transcript completeness; and source type (transcript, review, or supporting material). Mark absent, uncertain, contradictory, or incomparable metadata **Unknown** or **Uncertain**, state the effect on the comparison, and ask for clarification when it would change a conclusion. Do not convert a filename, folder name, historical context, or an external interview report into proof of an interview’s identity, order, stage, or candidate performance.

Keep real interviews and simulations in separate evidence sets. An earlier simulation is excluded from real-interview longitudinal conclusions and later-round preparation unless the user separately requests a comparison; even then, label it as training evidence and never treat simulated progress as proof of improvement in real interviews. External interview reports may affect likely question themes and preparation priority only; they do not evaluate the candidate’s performance or override selected real-interview evidence.

For a performance fact, an explicitly selected real transcript is direct evidence and an earlier review is supporting judgment. When they conflict, use the transcript for the factual claim, identify the conflicting review conclusion and relevant turns, and explain the effect on the result. Do not silently merge or choose between them.

## Longitudinal comparison workflow

Extract each selected interview independently before comparing it. For each record, cite turn, timestamp, question, or other interview-specific evidence for the following normalized dimensions when applicable:

| Dimension | What to normalize |
| --- | --- |
| Answer structure | conclusion placement, answer order, and directness |
| Concision | relevant length, repetition, and background load |
| Ownership | personal responsibility, decisions, and contribution boundaries |
| Metric credibility | baseline, definition, method, result, and limitations |
| Product judgment | problem framing, alternatives, prioritization, and decisions |
| AI understanding | AI necessity, data, evaluation, risks, cost, latency, and fallback |
| Technical collaboration | technical partnership, constraints, and implementation trade-offs |
| Trade-offs | explicit options, decision basis, and consequences |
| Stakeholder handling | alignment, conflict, influence, and decision ownership |
| Pressure response | response to challenge, contradiction, uncertainty, or follow-up |
| Self-awareness | failure, reflection, limits, and learning |
| Company fit | role motivation and connection to the supplied company/role evidence |
| Candidate questions | relevance, depth, and use of interview context |

Compare only like-enough observations. Group findings by available company, role, stage, interviewer function, question type, and difficulty before claiming change. For example, a technical deep dive in a later technical round is not directly comparable to an HR motivation question. If important controls differ or are unknown, report the comparison as conditional, inconsistent, or insufficient rather than producing a single overall score. Do not infer a cause from a pattern: place observed behavior and causal hypotheses in separate fields, and label each hypothesis with competing explanations.

Classify each dimension as one of the following:

- **Stable strength:** repeated, hiring-relevant strength across comparable interviews.
- **Stable weakness:** repeated, hiring-relevant weakness across comparable interviews.
- **Improving:** later comparable evidence is materially stronger than earlier evidence, with limits disclosed.
- **Regressing:** later comparable evidence is materially weaker than earlier evidence, with limits disclosed.
- **Inconsistent:** the evidence varies without a controlled explanation.
- **One-off:** a localized observation in a single interview; do not generalize it.
- **Insufficient evidence:** evidence is missing, not comparable, or too limited for a pattern claim.

Every stable, improving, regressing, or inconsistent pattern must cite at least two interview-specific pieces of evidence. A one-off needs its single citation and must remain local. Do not call a difference improvement or regression merely because interviews appear in a filename or folder sequence, or because a simulation went better than a real interview.

Prioritize findings by frequency, likely hiring impact, and ease of improvement. Produce a longitudinal output with: declared scope and exclusions; evidence register and metadata gaps; per-interview extraction; grouped comparison controls; dimension-by-dimension classifications with citations; observed behavior separated from hypotheses; priority table; and requests for facts needed to strengthen uncertain conclusions.

## Later-round preparation

Use selected earlier **real** interviews to prepare a current later round only when the user identifies the exact prior evidence to use. If a user merely says “prepare for the second round” without selecting earlier artifacts, follow the current preparation route using the explicitly supplied company, role, resume, and JD; say that no prior-round evidence was used, and request exact files or attachments before making claims about the earlier round. Do not scan a folder to find a presumed first round.

For designated earlier real evidence, extract direct positive hiring signals, unresolved doubts, repeated probes, contradictions, thin evidence, and items likely to receive an escalated verification. Treat the transcript as direct evidence and a prior review as supporting judgment; when they differ, report the discrepancy and base factual claims on the transcript. External interview reports may refine likely question themes, but cannot replace the earlier transcript’s evidence.

The later-round output must include:

1. The selected earlier evidence, exclusions, and metadata limits.
2. Positive signals already established in the earlier round.
3. Core doubts not yet resolved, repeated probes, contradictions, and weak evidence likely to be re-tested.
4. Conditional likely next-round focus, tied separately to earlier evidence and any external theme evidence.
5. A re-prioritized targeted question set for the current round.
6. Answer repairs that use only supported candidate facts and explicit factual placeholders for gaps.
7. An interviewer-adaptation strategy: use the earlier interviewer’s observable behavior as prior evidence only, form a fresh provisional hypothesis from the next interviewer’s first substantive questions, and revise it as evidence changes. Never assume the next interviewer shares the prior interviewer’s style.
8. Facts, current-round inputs, or selected artifacts still needed from the user.

Do not add a development plan by default. Only when the user asks for one, collect the next interview date and available practice time when those details would change the plan; do not assume a two-week window or an unrealistic cadence. If a two-week development plan is both requested and feasible, end with a two-week schedule mapping every priority to drills and measurable success criteria. If the interview is sooner, adapt the plan to the available period; if timing is unknown, ask before committing to a schedule.
