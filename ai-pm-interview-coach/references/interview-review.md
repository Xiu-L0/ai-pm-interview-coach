# Real-interview review

Apply this workflow only to a user-designated real-interview transcript or recording. Read the [input contract](input-contract.md) first. A filename, workspace folder, prior chat, training exercise, or simulation does not establish that an artifact is a real interview.

## Evidence gate and modality disclosure

Before analyzing performance, ask the user to designate the exact real-interview file or attachment if they have not already done so. Echo that exact designation in the report. State the direct materials analyzed, supporting material, material excluded from scoring, limitations, speaker/timestamp availability, and whether raw audio was actually processed. Do not merge a simulation into performance scoring. Use a prior simulation only for an explicitly requested comparison, in a separate comparison section.

Use this disclosure exactly, completing every bracket:

> **Modality disclosure:** Designated real-interview evidence: [exact user-designated file or attachment]. Analyzed: [timestamped transcript / plain transcript / audio / images]. Audio operation completed: [none / transcription / speaker separation / timestamp extraction / acoustic listening or analysis]. Unavailable or not processed: [modalities and files]. Limits: [what the available evidence cannot establish].

Decide raw-audio handling in this order:

1. Is the designated artifact explicit real-interview evidence? If not, ask the user which artifact is real and stop.
2. Is an audio capability available and has it successfully processed the designated audio? If yes, name the completed operation in the disclosure and use only claims that operation supports.
3. If audio processing is unavailable or fails, disclose that fact, request a speaker-labeled timestamped transcript, and continue only with the readable text already available.
4. An audio extension or a supplied MP3/WAV never proves decoding, transcription, listening, or acoustic analysis. Transcription supports words and perhaps timestamps; it does not support tone, emotion, vocal hesitation, or speaking-rate claims unless acoustic properties were separately analyzed.
5. Timestamped text supports elapsed duration, answer length, conclusion position, and visible interruption analysis. It does not establish vocal tone. Treat textual silence (an explicit `[silence]`, pause annotation, or a missing textual response) separately from audible silence, which requires successfully analyzed audio.

If files are unreadable, encrypted, unsupported, incomplete, speaker-ambiguous, or timestamp-disordered, identify the affected file and the resulting reliability limit. Analyze only the safe portion, mark uncertainty rather than repairing it, lower confidence where needed, and request the missing modality for any unsupported conclusion.

## Transcript reconstruction and coverage

Normalize turns for analysis while retaining the original wording, speaker labels, and timestamps. Mark uncertain speaker attribution rather than guessing. Separate interviewer main questions, meaningful follow-ups, candidate answers, answer corrections, interruptions, candidate counter-questions, and rapport, scheduling, or other meta-conversation. Meta-conversation may be retained in an appendix but never enters capability scoring.

For a long transcript, first create a complete chronological question index. Process bounded sections while preserving timestamps and cross-section references. Before finalizing, reconcile every substantive question, follow-up, answer, and correction against the index and disclose any unresolved segment. Never claim full-interview coverage after reading only a partial segment.

When a past review conflicts with the original designated transcript, treat the transcript as direct performance evidence. Name the discrepancy, cite the relevant turn(s), and explain its effect on the conclusion rather than silently adopting the old review.

## Per-question analysis

Analyze every substantive question with three layers: required core analysis, only the question-relevant adaptive dimensions, and open diagnosis for findings outside either structure. The fixed structure is a minimum, not a reason to fill irrelevant fields.

### Core analysis (required)

For each question, provide:

1. Question, timestamp, and interview stage when known.
2. Inferred intent, labeled as an inference.
3. A concise outline of the candidate answer, including any correction or material follow-up.
4. The interviewer’s subsequent reaction, follow-up, challenge, interruption, or lack of visible response.
5. What worked, with quoted or precisely located evidence.
6. What failed, with equally specific evidence.
7. Root cause, separating a content gap from an expression, evidence, ownership, or reasoning gap.
8. Severity and likely interviewer impact.
9. An improved answer strategy based only on candidate facts already supported by the record.
10. A concise rewritten answer; use factual placeholders or questions for missing facts rather than inventing them.
11. A targeted drill and likely next verification question.

### Adaptive dimensions

Add only dimensions relevant to the question, such as metric credibility, product judgment, technical depth, stakeholder handling, strategic framing, pressure response, ethics, or role fit. Project questions may require personal responsibility, goal, decision basis, results, authenticity, failure, and trade-offs. AI-product questions may require AI necessity, alternatives, data, evaluation, hallucination, cost, latency, fallback, and feedback loop. Pressure questions may require response to challenge, defensiveness, evasion, contradiction, and reflection quality.

### Open diagnosis and consistency

Record material findings not anticipated above—such as a contradiction, interaction shift, or repeated interviewer doubt—with its evidence, affected questions, confidence, and competing explanation. Run a cross-question check for ownership, metrics, chronology, product rationale, career motivation, repeated evasion, and interviewer doubts. Do not turn a single miss into a global pattern without repetition or decisive evidence.

## Severity, scoring, and expression diagnosis

Use a strict, evidence-grounded severity tied to probable hiring impact, never to tone or personality:

| Severity | Priority | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | P0 | Likely direct rejection risk: decisive authenticity, ownership, ethics, or core-role failure. |
| Major | P1 | Repeated or substantial weakness that materially lowers competitiveness. |
| Moderate | P2 | Local reasoning, structure, evidence, or expression issue. |
| Minor | P3 | Useful optimization with limited likely hiring impact. |

Score only dimensions relevant to the target role, using 1–5 when scoring makes the evidence clearer: 1 is a severe risk, 3 is minimally adequate, and 5 is an unusually strong hiring signal. Do not mechanically average dimensions. A decisive authenticity, ownership, or core-role risk must remain visible even where other dimensions are strong.

Diagnose expression from observable evidence: conclusion position, answer length and verbosity, repetition, filler words present in text, excessive background, missed question intent, weak or delayed personal ownership, unsupported metrics, shallow trade-offs, contradiction, jargon, evasion, response after challenge, and poor structure. Distinguish visible textual evidence from acoustic findings; duration inferred from timestamps is not acoustic analysis.

## Whole-interview conclusion and practice plan

Lead with a qualitative hiring signal—**strong pass signal**, **lean pass**, **borderline**, **lean reject**, or **strong reject signal**—and confidence (**high**, **medium**, or **low**). This describes the evidence in the interview, not a final hiring decision. Never output a pass probability, percentage, or pseudo-precise odds.

Then report decisive strengths, decisive risks, the most likely rejection reason, the most likely next-round verification, repeated patterns, missed opportunities, a contradiction list, expression diagnosis, candidate-question assessment, and the next three practice priorities. For every priority, include occurrences, affected questions, root cause, training method, and a verification standard. Direct positive and negative judgments must both cite evidence; do not add flattery, humiliation, or personality labels. If ambiguity prevents a finding, say so and request the missing evidence instead of filling the gap.
